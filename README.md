# Proceeding_Polarization_Metadata-2000-2026
This study systematically reviews political, social, psychological, economic, and media literature on polarization using global and Korean database metadata. Applying machine learning "Literature Hypergraph", it compares domestic and international trends, and proposes future research agendas for the 2026 &lt;Korea Speech and Communication Association> Fall Conference.


**Data Collection**

• English search terms: polarization, polarisation, polarized, polarised, polarizing, polarising, polarize(s), polarise(s), depolarization. (Added after discovering the omission of the verb form 'polarize/polarizes' in the 2026-10-11 pilot) Use the wildcard (?) with both the US and UK spellings. If you cut it as wide as polari*, it also captures polarity (the emotional polarity in text analysis), which increases noise, so it is not used.

• Korean search term: 양극화. In the Korean database, we include the English search term to find papers containing the English abstract or keyword 'polarization'.

• Field filter required: "polarization" is used overwhelmingly in physics (polarization of light), chemistry, life sciences (cell and macrocell polarization), and electrical engineering, so it must be limited to a specific field. Since multidisciplinary journals cannot be strictly divided by field, they are filtered once more using a social science context (Block B).


**Selection Criteria (Screening Sequence)** (need revision)

• To ensure systematic literature selection, publications are screened sequentially through a six-step process. 

•First, the study must be an empirical research article or a literature review published in an academic journal (excl:not-journal); inclusion of review articles is determined by research question Q2. 

•Second, eligible studies must have been published between 2000 and 2026 (excl:pre2000). 

•Third, candidate publications must possess a title, an abstract, and keywords (excl:no-abstract / excl:no-keywords), with acceptable keyword scopes defined under research question Q6. 

•Fourth, at least one of these three sections—title, abstract, or keywords—must explicitly contain the term "polarization" or its relevant Korean equivalents (excl:no-term). 

•Fifth, the usage of "polarization" must explicitly reflect its social science context, excluding unrelated homonyms such as light polarization or cellular polarization (excl:off-topic). Finally, the research must fall within media, politics, economics, or social welfare domains, or be published in designated interdisciplinary journals (excl:off-field).
